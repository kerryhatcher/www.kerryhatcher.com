As parents in Bibb County, we see things our schools do well and things we’d like them to do better. But what exactly makes one school more effective than another? That’s what Jessica H. Garcia wanted to find out in her 2014 Mercer University dissertation “My School Is Better Than Yours.” She picked two Georgia elementary schools that did well even with high poverty and lots of kids moving in and out. She found adults who expected a lot from the kids and gave them a school where they felt safe.

So what can we do with that here? I’m starting with Alexander II and Springdale. I’ll draw from research and reporting from 2014 through 2024. If there’s something you think I should see at your school, please tell me.

What’s ahead in this series?

Teamwork, that's what

Below is a preview of the upcoming topics we’ll be covering. These are the areas that research (and common sense) suggest can make a real difference in our schools. Each topic will include practical ideas for parents, community volunteers, and school leaders to help turn principles into action right here in Bibb County. We also may have some special topics that might be covered as a result of feedback or requests, so please send in your questions and comments!

Strong Leadership, Strong Schools

When the Alexander II parents turned out over a principal’s reassignment, it got me thinking about what you lose when a good principal goes. It’s worth asking the teachers what that person does for them every week.

Supporting Teachers

The teachers in Garcia’s study weren’t superheroes. They were regular people who were trusted to do their jobs. When they needed help, they had a principal to ask.

High Expectations for Every Student

High expectations are easy to talk about. What does a teacher do on Tuesday when someone’s still stuck on Monday’s work? What can we do at home?

A Caring School Climate

Remember being the new kid? A buddy helps. I want to hear what schools do when a kid needs a friend or when somebody is picking on them.

Engaging the Community: Volunteers & Partnerships in Action

Bibb is asking retired teachers to come back and read with kids. You don’t have to have a kid in school to do something for one. I’d like to find out who else has a couple hours to give.

Safe and Supportive: The Role of Campus Police in School Culture

If a kid needs help, would they go up to the officer at school? That’s a question I want to ask about campus police here.

Overcoming Challenges: Supporting Students Through Poverty and Mobility

It’s hard to start over halfway through a school year. That’s on top of whatever a family is already dealing with, like food, clothes, or after-school care. I’ll look at how often kids move between Bibb schools and what we do to help when they do.

Spotlight on Success: Lessons from Alexander II and others

I’ll end up back at Alexander II and other schools doing well here. I’ll look at scores, awards, and the state’s “Beat the Odds” ratings. Then I’d like to ask the people who work there what they do that others could try.

Why does this all matter?

Because every child in Bibb County deserves an excellent education, and we, parents, educators, and community members have the power to make our schools better. Over the coming weeks, as we unpack each of these topics, we invite you to join the conversation. Have an idea or example to share? Drop it in the comments or reach out. This series is meant to spark a community dialogue around our schools. Some of the data might show we have a long way to go, but by learning from research and from each other, we can take concrete steps toward improvement.

Stay tuned for the first full post in the series, and let’s work together to ensure all our local elementary schools become places we’re proud to say are getting better every day, maybe one day every school in Bibb County shining example to the rest of the state!
