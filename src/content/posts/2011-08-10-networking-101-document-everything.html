Documentation is your friend. Start before you touch the network. Draw what’s there, draw where you want it to go, and write down the steps between the two. Take pictures while you’re at it. The next person in that closet will want to know what you did and why. That person might be you.

Visio is your friend

Documentation, Documentation, Documentation, did I say Documentation?

Reading the network diagram. Existing fiber links City Hall, Fire Admin, the Annex, and Terminal Station. A firewall at Terminal Station connects the network to the internet. Planned fiber shows proposed connections, future fiber marks later expansion, and wireless links show connections that use radio.

My Windows recommendation in 2011 was Microsoft Visio. For an open-source option, I pointed readers to Dia, a Visio-like diagramming app.

Take lots of photographs

A picture is worth a thousand words.


When making your plans take lots of photographs of the current environment. This will come in handy during the planing stage. Trying to explain to someone how bad a network closet is isn't nearly as effective as showing them. These don't need to be from a fancy camera. In 2011, I found BlackBerry and iPhone cameras good enough for this, though frequent flash use could drain the battery fast.

Blogger

Layout your entire plan before hand

Yes, your design will change. No plan ever survives meeting the enemy but that is ok. At least you can see what changed and why, and then communicate with others.

Keep in mind that you are not the only one

No matter how big or small the job is, someone else will eventually have to figure out what you did and why. So please just print out the thing put it in an sheet protector and tape to the door.
